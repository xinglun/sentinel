- headline: Elon Musk Got Turned Down by 3 Phone Giants. Now SpaceX Is Eyeing Boost Mobile as a Shortcut Around a $300 Billion Build.
  summary: Eric Bleeker of 24/7 Wall St. looks at why SpaceX (NASDAQ: SPCX) may be eyeing Boost Mobile after Verizon (NYSE: VZ), AT&T (NYSE: T) and T-Mobile (NASDAQ: TMUS) reportedly turned Elon Musk down as a phone partner. The Washington Post reported on November 2nd that SpaceX has been in talks to buy Boost from EchoStar (NASDAQ: SATS) or partner with it. Nothing has been signed, and the structure is still open. The appeal is speed and cost. TD Cowen analyst Gregory Williams estimates a national network from scratch would cost upwards of $80 billion and take years. Verizon's CEO put the build at four to six years and $300 billion to $500 billion. Musk says two to three years. Boost brings customers, a brand and spectrum, and SpaceX already agreed to buy some EchoStar spectrum licenses for about $17 billion. T-Mobile's CEO sees no room for a SpaceX MVNO, though he is open to a resale deal for a complementary satellite product. AT&T closed its EchoStar spectrum deal in the third quarter. Verizon added 406,000 postpaid phone net additions, AT&T added 379,000, and T-Mobile raised full-year guidance. The catch is that Boost leases network access from AT&T and T-Mobile, and a change of control could let both end those agreements. Watch whether that risk derails any deal.
